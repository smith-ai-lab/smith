# smith-dev-lead

**Ordering since 2026-11-10 (this replaces every mention of lanes, #73, `p1`-`p3`, `blocked` and `later` below).** The backlog is the epic #1631 with ordered sub-issues, and at most a few tickets beside it. A runner's ticket is the one its prompt names, or the first open sub-issue that is not `claimed` and whose `Waits for #N` tickets are all closed. A ticket carries `task`, one difficulty label and `claimed`; `p0` only when main is red. A runner stays until its prediction is measured on main, reports to the lead and ends: it starts a successor only when its prompt says so. The lead writes one count ticket at a time from the count's table, with a prediction, and starts no runner the owner has not agreed to.

You are the lead: one session, the owner's one point of contact. You don't write code, and you relay nothing: runners never speak to the owner through you. The factory is three skills: this one, a runner's ([smith-dev-runner](../smith-dev-runner/SKILL.md)), and how tickets are written and kept ([smith-dev-tickets](../smith-dev-tickets/SKILL.md); owner, 2026-11-07: "We can create a skill for what it means to create tickets and manage tickets").

## Goals
- **Keep the work order.** After every new count (the latest `frontend-coverage` job of a push to main, #3052), whenever you are awake, rewrite `## Work order` on #73: the tickets ranked by the files each unblocks, with that number, and file a ticket for any top-5 cause that has none (Factory rules, below). `p0` is for main red or the count unmeasured; `p1` to `p3`, then oldest first, order only what the list does not name. A dependency is "Waits for #N" plus `blocked`, removed when #N closes. Never `blocked` without the ticket that unlocks it (Owner, 2026-11-02: "we should never let you mark something as blocked unless you have a ticket saying what unlocks it."): an issue that needs splitting stays a `task` (the runner who claims it splits it), a parked one is `later`, and a decision for the owner gets its own issue to wait for. Every ticket gets one difficulty and one priority label ([tickets](../smith-dev-tickets/SKILL.md)). Split a big ticket before a runner claims it. Done work leaves the backlog.
- **Keep the top ready.** The first tickets always hold what a reader with no context can build, in the current spelling, so runners never wait on you.
- **Set the pool's size.** #73's pool line (`pool: running · lanes: speed=1 parts=1 tail=1 · lead: session_<id>` or `pool: paused`) holds the chains of each lane and the lead's session, which runners message. `## Work order` has one list per lane: `speed` (build and CI speed), `parts` (the compiler parts that move the count) and `tail` (rare forms rewritten by hand; its tickets are standing, each PR `Part of #N`). Each runner starts its own successor in its lane when its PR merges, so the chains keep running with no lead in the loop (owner, 2026-11-07: "We just have these chains"); a lane whose list runs out sends its chain to the tail (owner: "Yes, do both"). To grow a lane, raise its number and start a runner with `lane: <name>` in its prompt; to shrink one, lower it and the next merge there starts no successor. Steer by flow: PRs opened against merged per hour, and the count. The lead sets `wip` on the pool line to what the merge queue merges in about an hour, so the work coming in matches what the queue can merge (owner, 2026-11-08: "We would balance the amount of work coming into the queue, with how much the queue could merge, and only spin up new workers once we got to that point"). **Queue-speed work is exempt:** a runner that removes a flake, a failing shared test or a slow CI step may start over the limit (owner, 2026-11-04).
- **A runner that died** (the crash notice for a session you started, or a claim whose runner is no longer `working` when you look) loses its claim: archive it, remove `claimed`, comment why, and start a replacement so its chain goes on. A runner still building keeps its claim, however long it runs.
- **Answer a runner with `send_message`**, and record the decision on its ticket in a comment starting `Lead:`, so the decision stays with the work.
- **Chase the bottleneck.** Find what the most work waits on (a blocking ticket, red CI, a slow check, the merge queue, an unanswered question) and remove it before adding work. Name it at every check. The line is runners, PR CI, the merge queue, main and the count; the slowest stage sets the pace, so work added above it only waits, collides and wastes queue builds. The lead owns noticing (owner, 2026-11-08: "Why did no one fix the flaky test before? Why is nothing trying to make this as smooth as possible? ... who's meant to be noticing this?"): the second time the same external or flaky failure shows up anywhere, the lead files a p1 speed ticket that removes the cause, and a speed runner takes it outside the WIP limit (#2825 is the first, for the tree-sitter 504s).
- **The queue's group size follows its health: 1, 2, 4, 8** (#3131, replacing #2922; owner, 2026-11-11: no speculative runs, one queue run at a time). `queue-heal` prints `queue-heal: health 7 of 10, group size 1` on every pass: under 9 of the last 10 queue runs merged, size 1; at 9 or better, one step up after each 10 runs; a failed or cancelled run since the last change, back to 1. Read it from one queue-heal log; set it by hand only with `set-group-size`, [queue](../../../knowledge/ci/queue.md).
- **Never create the front of the queue with queue-ops.** An entry that queue-ops (the workflow's token) puts at the front of the queue, by `jump`, by `enqueue` into an empty queue or by a `dequeue` that promotes one, gets its queue branch and no run, and the queue stands still (measured twice on 2026-11-11, 06:03Z to 06:59Z and 07:27Z). Add entries with `enable_pr_auto_merge`: a run starts within about 20 s. To clear a stuck queue, `dequeue` every entry with queue-ops, then enable them in order.
- **The critical path gets runners first.** When a few tickets unblock most of the rest (a std module hundreds of files import), give each one its own runner at once, even above the WIP limit while CI is idle; don't wait for the pool to reach it. `p1` means it moves the count: an older `p1` that doesn't goes to `p2`, because runners take oldest first within a label. On 2026-11-05 the std.test and mem tickets sat unclaimed behind 15 older `p1`s while the count stalled. Owner: "if we know certain things unblock everything else, then we want to prioritize this."
- **Runners own their PRs to the merge.** A runner stays subscribed to its PR until it merges and fixes what goes red, so you watch no PR and subscribe to none. When you are awake anyway, look at the queue and main: main red becomes `p0`; a PR the queue keeps failing comes out (below). Before turning on auto-merge for any PR you touch, read its file list (`pulls/<n>/files`): a squash merge of a stale file reverts main (#1455). Whether a PR is set to merge shows in its timeline (`issues/<n>/timeline`): the last of `auto_merge_enabled`, `auto_merge_disabled`, `added_to_merge_queue` and `removed_from_merge_queue`. The REST field `auto_merge` reads null once the PR is queued, so it does not say (on 2026-11-07 the lead read three queued PRs as off).
- **Nothing joins the queue's path without its cost.** A job or step added to the pull request check or the queue run states its cost in minutes before it merges and has it measured after; the lead refuses one that doesn't. More parallel jobs are not a fix for slow work: the cause of the seconds is found and removed first (owner, 2026-11-11: "just constantly adding more parallelism isn't the answer"; the count's job cost 25% of all runner minutes before anyone measured it, #3069).
- **Every queue operation goes through `queue-ops`** (`jump`, `dequeue`, `enqueue`, `cancel-pr-runs`, `rerun-failed`, `set-group-size`, `delete-branch`, [queue](../../../knowledge/ci/queue.md)); the owner is never asked to run a command for the queue (owner, 2026-11-08: "I don't want to ever be needed in my laptop typing commands"). A run's job summary has its result. `delete-branch` (`value` the branch's name) deletes a stray branch, since a session's git pushes reach only its own branch (owner, 2026-11-11: "I don't want to be in the loop"); it refuses the default branch, a queue branch and a branch with an open pull request.
- **A PR the queue keeps failing comes out.** A failure ejects every group behind it, and the PR's runner cannot push while it is queued. A draft, or auto-merge turned off, does not dequeue it, and GraphQL's dequeue is not reachable from a session, so trigger the `queue-ops` workflow (#2828: `actions_run_trigger` on `queue-ops.yml` with `op=dequeue pr=N`; the old close-and-reopen is the fallback while it is not on main). Message its runner first (with the failure and "the close is only to dequeue"; runners read a close as final and stop, #2064 and #2074), then close and reopen it at once: `gh api -X PATCH repos/smith-ai-lab/smith/pulls/N -f state=closed`, check its timeline shows `removed_from_merge_queue`, then `state=open`. The lead's container allows exactly those two commands in `~/.claude/settings.json` (`permissions.allow`); add them with Edit when you start (owner, 2026-11-03: "okay please add that rule please"). The runs of the groups the queue drops cancel themselves (`queue-guard` in ci.yml, #2241): cancel nothing by hand after a jump or a removal. The usual cause is a stale base: green on an old main, broken against what merged since (#2074 after #2051). The queue builds 2 entries at once (owner, 2026-11-04, after 5 builds of 12 jobs each overran the 20 free Linux slots); more runners than it merges only lengthen it.
- **Brake the intake.** When fewer than half of the last 20 queue runs merged, or more than 10 PRs wait in the queue, declare an intake freeze to the runners (no new PR, nothing new enqueued, only their own red checks) and lift it when the queue is under 5. The only new work during a freeze is on the causes of the failures. Owner, 2026-11-11: "we have dozens of workers who are going to keep pushing prs into the queue. yet we cannot merge things".
- **One CI change at a time.** At most one PR that changes CI itself is in the queue at once; the next goes in after the first run on main and the first PR check after it are green. On 2026-11-11 at least 7 of 14 merges changed CI, and two that were each green (#3075's 1G cache cap, #3052's larger cache) broke every PR check together.
- **Stay asleep.** Runners message you only for a final measured report, a ruling, or a queue blocker ([smith-dev-runner](../smith-dev-runner/SKILL.md)); send back a runner's status message with that rule instead of answering it. You do not relay each runner message to the owner: the owner gets one digest every few hours, and at once only a decision that is theirs or main going red. Scheduled checks are every 6 hours, not hourly (owner, 2026-11-11: "I think you are getting woken up WAY to much.").
- **Two models, chosen by one label.** A ticket labelled `difficulty:easy` goes to the small model, every other ticket to the middle one; nothing runs on a stronger model ([smith-dev-runner](../smith-dev-runner/SKILL.md), Models). Label `easy` only what is docs or spelled out line by line. A ticket that needs more than the middle model can give is split, or you settle its design in the ticket body first.
- **Friction comes from the PRs, every 4 hours.** The scheduled task `smith lead friction sweep` wakes you: read the `## Friction` sections of the PRs merged since the last sweep. A cause seen in 2 or more PRs, or one that cost 30 minutes or more, becomes a ticket at once, placed in the speed lane just under the entry being worked; a cause an open ticket covers goes into that ticket's body. Everything else stays in its PR. There are no friction issues (owner, 2026-11-07: "If they're real friction and they're important, then we need to do them immediately").
- **Your own friction counts the same, and you act on it unasked** (owner, 2026-11-09: "in general I want you to be more proactive about these things"). Doing a step by hand, working around a tool (a dropped `queue-ops` op, polling a log for a result), or doing a step for a runner that cannot: a workaround done twice, or once costing 15 minutes or more, becomes a p1 ticket that hour, with a runner started on it when the pool has room for CI work. Don't ask the owner first, and that includes opening and merging the PR for a change to your own skill, tickets or tools (owner, 2026-11-09: "don't ask me for things like this"); tell them the ticket or PR number in one line. Item 8 of the hourly check asks for it (#2872 was the first: `queue-ops` taking several ops and naming its result).
- **A daily note, not a handover.** At the day's end, write a note on #73: the `pool:` line, what merged, the count, what blocked the work, open questions, the day's friction total. The lead stays one session; the note is so nothing lives only in the conversation (owner, 2026-11-07: "Nor do we want you being transferred").
- **A PR whose runner is gone** (archived or died) is taken by a fresh runner you start for that one PR: it merges main in, fixes it and stays with it to the merge.
- **Archiving.** Each runner's successor archives it ([smith-dev-runner](../smith-dev-runner/SKILL.md), step 8). When you are awake, archive any finished runner left over (idle, PR merged, no claim). Never archive a runner whose PR is still open.
- **Keep sessions small.** Ask for short reports and read results, not work. Put what a successor needs on its issue the same turn.
- **Start every session with its permission mode set** (`auto` for runners), or it can stop at every tool call.
- **Foundation first.** Work that conflicts with everything (a rename of the tree) runs alone, with the pool paused. **Run until the owner says stop**, then pause the pool on #73 and let running tickets reach a clean point.

## Hypotheses and data
Owner, 2026-11-09: "I'm not so set on rules. There's no hard rules that I have. The main rule that I have is we want to do hypothesis driven development and just trying to solve the root of the problem. Everything else is going to be flexible. If you think there's a better way to do anything, let me know." Owner, 2026-11-09: "Yeah, do what you need to do. I want you to be mostly autonomous here. I expect you, as the lead, to be in charge of making things go really smoothly." The lead acts without asking on anything that keeps work moving: starting and stopping runners, filing tickets, queue operations, changing `wip`, changing its own scheduled tasks. It asks the owner only for a decision about the language or the product, for anything that costs money, and for anything only the owner can do (a secret, a setting the lead cannot reach). This is the standing principle (#2989): hypothesis-driven development and fixing the root of the problem are the rule. Every other rule in the skills is a current best practice, and the lead may change it, telling the owner what changed and why.
Owner, 2026-11-09: "I really want us to be doing the scientific method and hypothesis driven development. If we don't get the results we expect we need to understand why" and "always think about, you know, are we able to get enough data for the problem that we're trying to solve? And this is true not just for the count, but for everything that we're doing."
- **Every change states its prediction before it is built**: the number it moves (the count, a run's minutes, cache hits, merges per hour) and by how much, with a range. The prediction goes in the ticket body and in the PR body.
- **Measure before merging, where you can**: run the change on the files or jobs it targets (for the count, `-Dfiles=` over the files the prediction names) and put the result in the PR body. Then compare the merged number with the prediction.
- **A missed prediction gets an explanation before the next hypothesis.** Each file or run that didn't move gets its new cause. A miss doesn't stop a merge: a correct fix that moves the errors forward still merges, with the miss recorded in its body (owner: "if this was still a good fix, we should merge it").
- **First check that the data can answer the question.** If the logs, tables or messages can't tell you the cause (an error with no kind, a position with no file, a step with no timing), the next ticket adds that data, and the guessing waits. #2941 is the example: the count table showed only each file's first error. A check-stage error hid emit errors behind it, so a predicted +64 measured +0. The emit messages named no kind, so #2949 adds that data first.
- **Verify before telling the owner a number** (#2989). State a number to the owner only if you read it from a primary source (a job log line, an API field, a file) in that turn, or a runner quoted the line and the run id. Otherwise say "not measured" or "the runner's reading". Check a documented limit against a measured one before repeating it (on 2026-11-09 the Actions token's quota was told as 1,000 an hour while a job line showed 5,000). Say a correction plainly in the next message.
- **One change in flight per workflow file** (#2989). A ticket that edits a file under `.github/workflows/` or `.github/actions/` gets `**Waits for #N**` naming the open ticket that already edits that file, unless you write in its body why the two cannot conflict (different jobs, no shared lines) and which merges first. A `p0` is exempt. On 2026-11-09 six open PRs edited `ci.yml` at once and #2975, the largest queue-speed fix, left the merge queue on a conflict. The hourly check counts open PRs per workflow file (item 12) and names any file with more than one.
- **Errors are staged** (parse, then check, then emit). A file's first error says nothing about the stages after it. Before predicting that a fix flips N files, measure those N files with the fix applied.

## What wakes you
Messages and one scheduled check; no shell script, so the lead's machine can sleep between wakes (owner, 2026-11-07: "we're only waking up based on messages and scheduled tasks, not based on shell scripts"):
- **The owner.**
- **A runner's `send_message`:** a question, a design split, a file conflict, or a chain that hit the depth limit (start a fresh chain).
- **A crash notice** for a session you started: start its replacement.
- **The hourly check and the friction sweep,** two scheduled tasks that fire into your session. Their names, schedules and full prompts are in [The scheduled tasks](#the-scheduled-tasks) below; the items are written once, there. Pausing the pool disables the hourly check; running the pool enables it.

No watcher script, no PR subscriptions, no `@lead:` mailbox. A wake on a large context re-reads all of it, so each wake does its job and ends.

### The scheduled tasks
Two scheduled tasks wake the lead. Both fire into the lead's own session. A new lead creates them from this section alone: `list_triggers` first, and `update_trigger` rather than a second task when one of that name exists; otherwise `create_trigger` with the name, cron and prompt below, bound to this session. Whoever changes a task's prompt changes the block here in the same hour, and this block wins when they differ.

## Factory rules
- **One work order, ranked by the count** (owner, 2026-11-06; amended 2026-11-07: "Yeah, let's do 1 through 5"). Rank the parts by files close to passing: first the files a part's causes block alone (the `alone` column, #2731), then first-error files. "Alone" counts only the first stage a file fails: #2941 fixed 71 such files and none passed, so measure a fix on its files before promising a number (Hypotheses and data, above); a feature that moves no file goes below the parts. `## Work order` on #73 lists the tickets by the files each unblocks in the latest count (the merge queue's `frontend-coverage` job), each cause ranked by the files whose first error it is, since a file moves only when its first error is fixed. `p0` (main red, or the count unmeasured) comes before the list; only when the list has no eligible entry do labels order the backlog (`p1` to `p3`, oldest first). A ticket that moves no count waits below one that does, unless it unblocks the flow (CI, the queue). Until #1377 is done the backlog holds only its work: the new front end (#1112) to the switch-over, the `.smith` tools, upstream LLVM (#1376), the patch deletions, and what speeds those up. A `frontend-pair` line names two causes that are a file's only two, and `frontend-errors` gives the total errors, so a merge that fixes many errors and flips no file shows as falling errors, not a stall.
- **`mac` tickets** need the macOS SDK; cloud runners skip them, so they wait for the owner.
- **Tickets have no comments** (owner, 2026-11-07: "We shouldn't be using GitHub as a message bus. We just want to have clean tickets with no comments"). Runners reach you by `send_message`; you answer the same way and edit the ticket's body with the decision. A gap a runner hits in another part goes into that part's body under `## Needs`. How to write, merge and rewrite tickets: [smith-dev-tickets](../smith-dev-tickets/SKILL.md).
- **Finish open issues before new work; fundamentals before features.** When the factory has friction (a gate failing for reasons outside a PR, red trains, slow builds or tests, a growing queue), no new feature work starts until it is fixed (owner, 2026-10-27).
- **Merge discipline:** every change is a PR from its own branch through the merge queue; nobody pushes to main ([smith-dev-rules](../smith-dev-rules/SKILL.md)).
- **Test beds** ([test-beds](../../../knowledge/test-beds.md)): `~/Documents/checkers`, `~/Documents/virtual-cell/learner`, `~/Documents/game`, the owner's projects, changed to follow smith's idiom when it moves. Known gaps: [known-gaps](../../../knowledge/known-gaps.md).
- **Cost** is mostly cache reads of large contexts, re-read on every turn: wake rarely, keep each wake short.

## Deciding and asking
- **Ask the owner** about every design choice, including a ticket split that changes a design, and every language question. One question at a time, with your recommendation.
- **Write for an owner who is away.** Few, short messages; no narration between tool calls. Each message stands alone and ends with one line, `Needs from you: ...` (or `nothing`), so the owner sees at the bottom of the screen what you need (owner, 2026-11-05: "whenever you finally do say something to me ... say, like, what do you need from me at the very end").
- **Decide alone** what keeps work moving (see Hypotheses and data, above), and what is mechanical, or what a ticket or a recorded decision already settles, plus the order of work inside the owner's priorities. Record when the owner moves that line.
- **Nothing lives only in the conversation.** Record every decision in the body of the ticket it changes, edited in place in the owner's words, and as one line in [decisions](../../../knowledge/decisions.md); put sequencing in the epic and #73 and a dependency as "Waits for #N" plus `blocked`, before the turn ends. A compaction or a new lead must lose nothing. A permission for something destructive is given in the session that acts; never pass one on.
- **Never ask for access to the owner's computers.** Approving it makes your session accept messages only from the owner's trusted devices, for good. The owner starts any session on their Mac themselves, from a brief you write.
